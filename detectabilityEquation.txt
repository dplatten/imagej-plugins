My original radial d' formulation:

d'^{2}=\frac{\left [  \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).E^{2}(f)df  \right ]^{2}}

{\int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).NPS(f).E^{4}(f)df}


d'=\frac{ \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).E^{2}(f)df }

{\left [ \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).NPS(f).E^{4}(f)df \right ]^{0.5}}





Updated equation that is mathematically equivalent to the 2d version - this has a 2.pi.f term within the numerator and
denominator integrals. The original version, without the 2.pi.r terms, does not correctly account for the increasing
number of fourier components at larger radial frequencies.

d'^{2}=\frac{\left [ 2\pi \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).E^{2}(f).fdf  \right ]^{2}}

{2\pi \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).NPS(f).E^{4}(f).fdf}


This can be simplified to:

d'^{2}=2\pi\frac{\left [  \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).E^{2}(f).fdf  \right ]^{2}}

{\int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).NPS(f).E^{4}(f).fdf}


Square rooting both sides to give just d' on the left hand side:

d'=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{ \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).E^{2}(f).fdf }

{\left [ \int_{0}^{f_{N}}\left | W(f) \right |^{2}.TTF^{2}(f).NPS(f).E^{4}(f).fdf \right ] ^{0.5}}
